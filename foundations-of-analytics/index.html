Beyond the episodes: the graded work

Learning Practices (40%)

Short graded assignments at the end of most modules — analytical problem-solving with data, models, and business scenarios. Most allow multiple attempts, so use them to find gaps before exams.

Decision Labs (20%)

Two small-group projects: analyze a real business analytics problem, use AI tools responsibly to explore alternatives, and submit a mini-project summary with your recommended decision.

Knowledge Checks (6%)

Quick quizzes after each block of module material — they keep you exam-ready. The checkpoint questions in each episode here are your practice arena for them.

Discussions (4%)

Connect course concepts to real-world examples and current events, share articles, and react to classmates — analytics gets more interesting outside the textbook.

What you'll be able to do (course learning outcomes)

CLO 1

Explain the role of data analytics in organizational decision-making and identify common analytics tools used in business.

CLO 2

Analyze datasets and apply basic analytical techniques using spreadsheet tools to support business decisions.

CLO 3

Interpret analytic models and communicate data-driven insights through professional reports and presentations.

CLO 4

Apply data analytics methods to evaluate real-world business problems and recommend evidence-based solutions.

Playing fair: AI & academic honesty

Generative AI is part of the modern analytics workflow, and this course treats it that way: use tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, or Copilot to ask questions, get explanations, and brainstorm approaches — but never to produce assignment content you submit as your own work, including exam answers. Decision Labs explicitly invite responsible AI use, with the tools you used described in your summary. All submitted work must be your own unless collaboration is explicitly allowed; when in doubt about an AI use, ask first.
