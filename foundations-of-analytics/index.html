The big idea

This course is not a formula-memorization exercise. It is about getting comfortable thinking with data — even in the moment someone says the word “model” and the room goes quiet.

The journey runs in three questions: what happened? → what will happen? → what should we do? Along the way you build confidence with the tools, and you practice explaining results the way a person would — not the way a spreadsheet would.

Where it gets tricky — and why that's normal

Data is not the same as information

A chart you cannot explain has not told you anything yet. Every exercise here asks “so what?” and “what would you decide?” — because interpretation is the skill, not the chart.

Spreadsheets feel overwhelming at first

Formulas, cell references, sorting, filtering, pivoting — a lot at once. The early episodes build these step by step, and repetition beats a single tutorial. Messy-data cleanup practice is part of the game.

Models can feel like sealed boxes

The way through is to build small ones yourself, change the inputs, watch what breaks, and explain the result in plain words. A model you can explain is a model you can trust — and question.

Describing is easier than predicting or prescribing

Most learners stall at “here's what happened.” The episodes carry the same business story through all three questions, so each jump feels gradual instead of sudden.

Numbers anxiety is common, even here

The emphasis is on reading results, not memorizing formulas. Visual explanations and real scenarios carry the weight, and early confusion is expected — it is a starting point, not a verdict.

Playing fair: AI & academic honesty

AI tools are part of how analysts work today, and the course welcomes them in that spirit: use tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, or Copilot to ask questions, get explanations, and brainstorm approaches — but never to write up work you hand in as your own, exam answers included. Decision Labs explicitly invite responsible AI use, with the tools you used described in your summary. Submitted work must be your own unless collaboration is explicitly allowed; when an AI use feels borderline, ask first.
